# Acalypha Indica

## Quick ID (field-use)
- Leaf type: Deciduous, broadleaf annual herb
- Leaf arrangement: Alternate
- One-liner ID clue: Upright annual weed with ovate, serrated leaves and green axillary flower spikes

## Taxonomy
- Family: Euphorbiaceae
- Genus: Acalypha

## Leaves
- Shape: Ovate to broadly ovate
- Margin: Serrate to crenate
- Venation: Pinnate with a prominent midrib and lateral veins

## Bark
- Texture: Herbaceous (non-woody)
- Color: Green
- Changes with age: Stems may become slightly fibrous but remain soft

## Reproductive Structures
- Flowers / cones:
	- Monoecious
	- Small, Inconspicuous green flowers in axillary spikes
	- Female flowers near base, male flowers toward tip
- Fruit / seeds:
	- 3-lobed capsules containing small seeds

## Size & Form
- Typical height: 30-75 cm
- Crown shape: Upright, bushy herb

## Habitat & Ecology
- Soil preference: Disturbed soils; loamy to sandy
- Moisture: Moderate; tolerates seasonal dryness
- Light: Full sun to partial shade

## Native Range
- Geographic distribution: Africa and South Asia

## Similar Species
- [[Acalypha Australis]]
- [[Acalypha Chamaedrifolia]]

## Medicinal Uses
- Leaf juice useful for loosening mucus and helping with cough/asthma
- Mild laxative
- Used for intestinal worms (anthelmintic)
- Sometimes used for indigestion
- Poultices can be used for joint pain, swelling, and rheumatic complaints
- Crushed leaves can be used for Eczema, scabies, and fungal skin infections

## Notes
- Common weed of roadsides, fields, and gardens
- Used in traditional medicine 
- Known host for [[Aecidium Acalyphae]]
- Identification relies on leaf shape and axillary inflorescences
