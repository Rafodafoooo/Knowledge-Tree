# Aecidium Abscedens

## Quick ID (high yield)
- Morphology: Rust fungus forming orange to yellow aecia on host plant tissues; produces echinulate aeciospores
- One-liner ID clue: Aecial rust producing blister-like orange pustules on plant surfaces

## Classification
- Phylum: Basidiomycota
- Typical form at 25°C: Filamentous rust fungus (aecial stage)
- Typical form at 37°C: No growth / not applicable

## Microscopy
- Key features:
	- Thick-walled, echinulate aesciospores
	- Spores yellow to orange-brown, Ellipsoid to Ovoid
	- Aecia Lined by Polygonal Peridial Cells
- Look-alikes: Other Aecidium Species

## Culture Characteristics
- Media: Not culturable on routine artificial media
- Colony description: N/A (Obligate Biotroph)
- Growth rate: N/A

## Habitat & Exposure
- Reservoir: Infected Host Plants
- Exposure route: Environmental plant-to-plant spread via airborne aeciospores

## Pathogenicity
- Virulence factors:
	- Obligate Biotrophy
	- Haustoria Nutrient Absorption
- High-risk patients: None (Plant Pathogen)

## Diseases Caused
- Plant Rust Disease

## Diagnosis
- Best tests: Field observation of aecia, Light microscopy of spores
- Typical results: Orange/Yellow Aecial Pustules, Echinulate/Thick Walled Aeciospores

## Treatment
- First-line: Not applicable (plant disease management only)
- Alternatives: Removal of infected plant material; forestry/agricultural control
- Resistance notes: Not applicable

- Related drug class: None
