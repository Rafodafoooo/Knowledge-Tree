# Acalypha Chamaedrifolia

## Quick ID (field-use)
- Leaf type: Evergreen, broadleaf
- Leaf arrangement: Alternate
- One-liner ID clue: Prostrate, mat-forming copperleaf with small, round, scalloped leaves and creeping stems

## Taxonomy
- Family: Euphorbiaceae
- Genus: Acalypha

## Leaves
- Shape: Orbicular to broadly ovate
- Margin: Crenate (rounded teeth)
- Venation: Palmate to weakly pinnate (veins radiate from leaf base)

## Bark
- Texture: Herbaceous to semi-woody at base
- Color: Green to reddish-green
- Changes with age: Older stems may become slightly woody and brown at nodes

## Reproductive Structures
- Flowers / cones:
	- Monoecious
	- Very small, Inconspicuous Green Flowers
	- Short axillary spikes, often hidden by foilage
- Fruit / seeds:
	- Small capsules with tiny seeds, rarely noticed

## Size & Form
- Typical height: 10-20 cm
- Crown shape: Prostrate, Spreading groundcover, trailing habit

## Habitat & Ecology
- Soil preference: Well-drained soils; tolerates poor soils
- Moisture: Moderate; drought-tolerant once established
- Light: Full sun to partial shade

## Native Range
- Geographic distribution: Native to Tropical and Subtropical Americas

## Similar Species
- [[Acalypha Reptans]]
- [[Acalypha Australis]]

## Notes
- Commonly used as an ornamental groundcover
- Not frost tolerant
- Can server as a host for [[Aecidium Acalyphae]]
- Easily identified by its creeping habit and round leaves
