# Acalypha Neomexicana

## Quick ID (field-use)
- Leaf type: Simple
- Leaf arrangement: Alternate
- One-liner ID clue: Small desert herb with soft, ovate, toothed leaves and slender, green, catkin-like flower spikes.

## Taxonomy
- Family: Euphorbiaceae
- Genus: Acalypha

## Leaves
- Shape: Ovate to broadly ovate
- Margin: Serrate to crenate
- Venation: Pinnate, clearly visible midrib with lateral veins

## Bark
- Texture: N/A (herbaceous; no true bark)
- Color: Green stems, sometimes reddish
- Changes with age: Stems become slightly tougher and more fibrous late season

## Reproductive Structures
- Flowers / cones: Inconspicuous, green, unisexual flowers in slender axillary spikes (male and female separate but on same plant)
- Fruit / seeds: Small, 3-lobed capsules typical of Acalypha

## Size & Form
- Typical height: 10–40 cm (4–16 in)
- Crown shape: Low, spreading to loosely upright

## Habitat & Ecology
- Soil preference: Sandy or gravelly soils; often disturbed sites
- Moisture: Dry to semi-dry
- Light: Full sun to partial sun

## Native Range
- Geographic distribution: Southwestern United States (especially New Mexico, Arizona, west Texas) and adjacent northern Mexico

## Similar Species
- [[Acalypha rhomboidea]]
- [[Acalypha ostryifolia]]

## Notes
- Name literally means New Mexico Copperleaf
- Common in desert grasslands and open scrub
- Easily overlooked due to non-showy flowers, leaves are best field cue.
