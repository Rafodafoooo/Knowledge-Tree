# Acalypha Hispida

## Quick ID (field-use)
- Leaf type: Evergreen, broadleaf shrub
- Leaf arrangement: Alternate
- One-liner ID clue: Tropical shrub with large ovate leaves and long, drooping, red, fuzzy flower spikes

## Taxonomy
- Family: Euphorbiaceae
- Genus: Acalypha

## Leaves
- Shape: Ovate to broadly ovate
- Margin: Serrate
- Venation: Pinnate, prominent midrib with lateral veins

## Bark
- Texture: Woody shrub; young stems pubescent
- Color: Green to brown
- Changes with age: Becomes more woody and darker with maturity

## Reproductive Structures
- Flowers / cones:
	- Monoecious
	- Female inflorescences are long, pendulous, bright red, and densely pubescent
- Fruit / seeds:
	- Small capsules, rarely produced in cultivation

## Size & Form
- Typical height: 1.5 - 3 m
- Crown shape: Rounded to spreading shrub

## Habitat & Ecology
- Soil preference: Fertile, well-drained soils
- Moisture: Moderate to high; prefers consistent moisture
- Light: - Full sun to partial shade (best flowering in bright light)

## Native Range
- Geographic distribution: New Guinea and Surrounding Regions

## Similar Species
- [[Acalypha Wilkesiana]]
- [[Acalypha Chamaedrifolia]]

## Notes
- Grown primarily as an ornamental
- Sensitive to cold, frost tender
- Can act as host to [[Aecidium Acalyphae]]
- Easily recognized by long red chenille-like catkins
