# Acalypha Ostryifolia

## Quick ID (field-use)
- Leaf type: Simple
- Leaf arrangement: Alternate
- One-liner ID clue: Weedy annual with thin, ovate, sharply toothed leaves and long, narrow green flower spikes arising from leaf axils.

## Taxonomy
- Family: Euphorbiaceae
- Genus: Acalypha

## Leaves
- Shape: Ovate to lance-ovate
- Margin: Sharply serrate
- Venation: Pinnate, prominent midrib with fine lateral veins

## Bark
- Texture: N/A (herbaceous annual)
- Color: Green to reddish-green
- Changes with age: Stems become tougher and slightly woody near the base late in the season

## Reproductive Structures
- Flowers / cones: - Inconspicuous, green, unisexual flowers in slender axillary spikes; male flowers usually above female flowers on the same spike
- Fruit / seeds: Small, 3-lobed capsules; seeds brown to dark, smooth

## Size & Form
- Typical height: 30 - 90 cm (1-3 ft)
- Crown shape: Upright to loosely spreading, branched

## Habitat & Ecology
- Soil preference: Disturbed Soils, loam to sandy substrates
- Moisture: Dry to moderately moist
- Light: Full sun to partial shade

## Native Range
- Geographic distribution: Eastern and central United States; extends into the Midwest and Southeast

## Similar Species
- [[Acalypha rhomboidea]]
- [[Acalypha virginica]]

## Notes
- Commonly called the Three-Seeded Mercury or Hophornbeam copperleaf
- Frequently encountered as an agricultural or garden weed
- Not edible, contains irritating compounds.
