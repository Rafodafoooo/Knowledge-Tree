# Aecidium Abietis-Mariesii

## Quick ID (high yield)
- Morphology: Rust fungus producing elongated orange aecia on fir needles; echinulate aeciospores
- One-liner ID clue: Aecial rust on Abies needles with orange blister-like lesions

## Classification
- Phylum: Basidiomycota
- Typical form at 25°C: Filamentous Rust Fungus (Aecial Stage)
- Typical form at 37°C: No Growth / Not Applicable (Plant Pathogen)

## Microscopy
- Key features:
	- Thick-Walled, Echinulate Aeciospores
	- Ellipsoid to Ovoid Spores, Yellow-Orange
	- Polygonal Peridial Cells Forming Aecial Wall
- Look-alikes: Other Rust Fungi

## Culture Characteristics
- Media: Not Routinely Culturable on Artifical Media
- Colony description: N/A (Obligate Biotroph)
- Growth rate: N/A

## Habitat & Exposure
- Reservoir: Fir Trees, Especially [[Abies Mariesii]]
- Exposure route: Plant-to-Plant via airborne Aeciospores (Environmental)

## Pathogenicity
- Virulence factors:
	- Obligate Biotrophy
	- Specialized Infection Structures (Haustoria)
- High-risk patients: None (Not Human Pathogen)

## Diseases Caused
- None to Humans
- Fir Needle Rusting

## Diagnosis
- Best tests: Macroscopic Examination of Needles, Light Microscopy of Aeciospores
- Typical results: Orange Aecia on Needles, Echinulate/Thick Walled Aeciospores

## Treatment
- First-line: N/A
- Alternatives: Remove infected plant material
- Resistance notes: N/A

## Links
- Related disease: Plant Rust Disease
- Related drug class: None
