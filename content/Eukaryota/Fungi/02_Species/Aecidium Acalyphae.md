# Aecidium Acalyphae

## Quick ID (high yield)
- Morphology: Orange-Yellow Aecia forming blister-like pustules on leaves and young stems, produce echinulate aeciospores
- One-liner ID clue: Aecial Rust on Acalypha with orange pustules

## Classification
- Phylum: Basidiomycota
- Typical form at 25°C: Filamentous rust fungus (aecial stage)
- Typical form at 37°C: No growth / not applicable

## Microscopy
- Key features:
	- Thick walled, echinulate (spiny) aeciospores, polygonal peridial cells forming the aecial wall
- Look-alikes: Other Aecidium species

## Culture Characteristics
- Media: Not culturable on routine artificial media
- Colony description: N/A (Obligate Biotroph)
- Growth rate: N/A

## Habitat & Exposure
- Reservoir: [[Acalypha]] (Species Euphoribiaceae)
- Exposure route: Environmental, plant-to-plant via airborne aeciospores

## Pathogenicity
- Virulence factors: Obligate biotrophy; haustoria for nutrient uptake
- High-risk patients: None (not a human pathogen)

## Diseases Caused
- Acalypha Rust

## Diagnosis
- Best tests: Field observation + light microscopy
- Typical results: Orange aecia on leaves/stems; echinulate aeciospores

## Treatment
- First-line: Not applicable (plant disease management)
- Alternatives: Remove infected material; horticultural/forestry controls
- Resistance notes: Not applicable

## Structural Biology
- Cell Wall - Chitin and Beta Glucans
- Spore Wall is thick, echinulate, pigmented
- Aecium architecture - Cup structure with peridium made of tightly packed polygonal peridial cells that rupture the plant epidermis
- Septation
## Cell Biology
- Obligate Biotroph
- Forms Haustoria inside host tissues
